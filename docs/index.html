Lilly's amylin-Zepbound combo posts best-ever weight loss in patients with obesity and diabetes

BioSpace / STAT News / Endpoints · 2026-09-30 · ⏱ 4 min read

Eli Lilly's EloraTZP — combining tirzepatide (Zepbound) with amylin agonist eloralintide — achieved 23.3% average weight loss at 48 weeks versus 14.8% for tirzepatide alone in a Phase 2b trial of 367 adults with obesity and type 2 diabetes, sweeping all primary and secondary endpoints including A1C reduction. The triple-pathway approach carries a tolerability caveat: discontinuation rates due to adverse events ranged 10.8–27% versus 2.9% for Zepbound alone, though Lilly attributes this partly to simultaneous dose escalation of two agents and plans an optimized escalation schedule in Phase 3. Phase 3 is set to launch in Q4 2025, and the data directly benchmarks against Novo's CagriSema, which beat Zepbound in a recent T2D trial but trails EloraTZP's headline weight loss number.

💡 With LLY establishing a new efficacy ceiling in obesity/T2D and Phase 3 imminent, the data reshapes the competitive GLP-1/amylin landscape — relevant for positioning in LLY, NVO, and smaller amylin-program names like Structure Therapeutics — while the safety overhang on discontinuation rates is the key risk variable to monitor heading into Phase 3 design disclosures.

MBI Deep Dives: The Case for Meta Enterprise Platform

MBI Deep Dives (Substack) · 2026-09-30 · ⏱ 8 min read

MBI Deep Dives builds a contrarian investment case for Meta's newly announced Enterprise Platform, arguing the move is strategically rational as a 'second customer' demand sink to absorb surplus compute capacity and reduce the cost of being wrong on a massive capex build. The piece examines how Meta's distribution scale, first-party AI products like Muse, and infrastructure investments position it to compete with entrenched enterprise players like Microsoft and Salesforce. 💡 For investors, this reframes Meta's enterprise push not as a distraction but as a compute-economics hedge that improves the risk/reward of its ~tens-of-gigawatts capex commitment — with meaningful implications for Meta's margin trajectory and competitive positioning against hyperscalers and AI-native players like OpenAI.
