GSK, Ionis win US approval for first-of-its-kind hepatitis B drug

BioPharma Dive · 2026-10-21 · ⏱ 3 min read

The FDA approved Hibsago (bepirovirsen), a GSK/Ionis collaboration, as the first treatment capable of achieving a functional cure for chronic hepatitis B — a disease affecting ~300 million people globally. In two Phase 3 trials, roughly 20% of patients on Hibsago plus antivirals achieved functional cure vs. 0% on placebo plus antivirals, with results published in NEJM. Jefferies projects ~$3B in peak annual sales, though mandatory weekly lab monitoring for liver inflammation and low platelets may slow broad commercial uptake.

💡 A landmark FDA approval for GSK and IONS with $3B peak sales potential, but the safety monitoring burden creates a real commercial overhang worth tracking as pricing and payer dynamics unfold.

📊 Slot 2

Cencora to acquire majority stake in $2.4B American Oncology Network

Fierce Healthcare · 2026-10-21 · ⏱ 4 min read

Cencora is acquiring a 75% majority stake in American Oncology Network, a 21-state oncology MSO with 350+ providers, in a deal valuing AON at $2.4 billion — its second major oncology network acquisition following the $5 billion OneOncology deal closed late 2025. Evercore analysts highlight AON's complementary positioning (focused on smaller community oncology practices vs. OneOncology's larger practices), limited integration risk given Cencora's existing 4% stake and distribution partnership since 2020, and project EPS accretion of $0.10 and $0.22 in FY2028 and FY2029 respectively. The deal extends a broader specialty practice network arms race among drug distributors, with Cardinal Health and McKesson also making billion-dollar moves in oncology, GI, and ophthalmology networks.

💡 Cencora's vertical integration into oncology MSOs is reshaping drug distribution economics and GPO dynamics in specialty pharma, with compounding EPS accretion implications and a clear competitive moat being built against Cardinal Health and McKesson.

🤖 Slot 3

Using thousands of AI agents to uncover new leads in biological data

OpenAI (via Hacker News) · 2026-10-21 · ⏱ 5 min read

OpenAI, PerturbAI, and Scripps Research deployed thousands of GPT-Rosalind-powered AI agents in parallel to analyze a whole-brain CRISPR atlas spanning millions of mouse brain cells, completing in 32 minutes what would have taken human researchers months. Each agent investigated a specific gene disruption, searched scientific literature, and proposed biological mechanisms; outputs were aggregated into a 'reasoning atlas' linking genes, cellular effects, and disease hypotheses. The distributed approach dramatically outperformed a single-agent baseline — citing 700+ references versus 11 — and surfaced novel leads including cerebellar-specific TSC2 effects and a potential cholesterol-neurodegeneration connection relevant to Alzheimer's and Parkinson's research.

💡 This case study concretely illustrates how agentic AI scaling and distributed biological reasoning are compressing the drug discovery hypothesis-generation cycle from months to minutes — a structural shift with direct implications for biotech R&D productivity and competitive moat analysis in AI-enabled research platforms.
