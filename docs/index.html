FDA rejects Summit, Akeso's ivonescimab in narrow lung cancer setting

Endpoints News · 2026-11-15 · ⏱ 4 min read

The FDA issued a complete response letter rejecting Summit Therapeutics and Akeso's ivonescimab for EGFR-mutated NSCLC, citing insufficient overall survival benefit from the HARMONi trial despite a 48% reduction in disease progression risk. The rejection blocks what would have been the first U.S. approval of a PD-1xVEGF bispecific antibody — a class with potential to rival checkpoint inhibitors like Keytruda — and leaves Summit without a resubmission timeline. The class story is not over, however, as the broader HARMONi-3 first-line data readout remains a future catalyst.

A Layman Thinks About AI (Howard Marks memo)

Oaktree Capital / Brookfield · 2026-11-15 · ⏱ 18 min read

Howard Marks structures AI investing around 11 key questions — covering what AI can actually do, who profits, data-center demand, credit market concentration risk, and whether we're in a bubble — while using Claude to score the predictability of AI sub-sectors (frontier labs score 2/10). He highlights Goldman Sachs data showing global AI-related debt issuance approaching $600 billion in 2026 and warns that tech equities, data-center loans, and IG bonds may all share correlated compute-demand risk. His conclusion is 'calibrated humility' on position sizing, arguing that compounding uncertainty across technology, demand, competition, and timing dramatically lowers the probability that any single AI investment thesis holds.

Anthropic vs Meta: Room to Be Wrong

MBI Deep Dives · 2026-11-15 · ⏱ 14 min read

MBI Deep Dives compares Anthropic (expected IPO at ~$1–1.5T) and Meta (~$1.5T EV) by decomposing each company's core business value versus its speculative AI bet, finding that ~60-67% of Meta's capex and a rising share of Anthropic's R&D are funding unproven products. The piece introduces a 'room to be wrong' margin-of-safety framework, tracking metrics like revenue per R&D dollar (which peaked at $1.91 for Anthropic in 2Q'26 before slipping), compute allocation between paying customers vs. research, and METR's finding that Anthropic's capability gains accelerated 1.9x with ~80% attributable to AI-assisted research. It also explores whether Meta's open-source strategy and ads-funded compute moat structurally compresses the addressable revenue pool for closed-model labs like Anthropic.
