📊 Slot 2

New memo: Shall We Repeal the Laws of Economics (Part III)

Howard Marks / Oaktree (via Brookfield newsletter) · 2026-09-23 · ⏱ 10 min read

Howard Marks argues that the Treasury's August 2025 announcement to double long-dated bond buybacks (up to $4B per operation) is a cosmetic intervention that cannot solve the structural drivers of rising long-term rates: persistent inflation (PCE at 3.7%), chronic fiscal deficits during prosperity, and multi-trillion-dollar capital demand from AI infrastructure and routine economic needs. Drawing on Druckenmiller's critique and the 1992 Soros/BOE trade as historical proof that markets ultimately defeat governments defending prices against fundamentals, Marks contends the 30-year yield's 19-year high reflects genuine economic forces that only fiscal discipline — not market manipulation — can durably address. He concludes that the confluence of federal deficits, private capital needs, and AI investment creates a structurally higher cost of capital that investors must price in.

💡 A credible, data-grounded case from one of fixed income's most respected practitioners that the rate-higher-for-longer regime is structural — not cyclical — has direct implications for biotech/healthcare valuations, leveraged health system financing, and any growth-oriented portfolio exposed to duration risk.
