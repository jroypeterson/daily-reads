🤖 Slot 3

Language Models Can Rewrite Their Own Weights

arXiv (via Hacker News) · 2026-11-21 · ⏱ 6 min read

Researchers demonstrate 'self-rewriting,' in which a 27B language model uses its own reasoning (no gradients, no retraining) to directly modify its own weight matrices after diagnosing its failures on graded answers. Over eight consecutive sessions, the model's accuracy improves monotonically, and it can even restore a damaged copy of itself to over 80% of original accuracy on unseen tasks — without ever reading the original weights. The capability scales with model size: smaller models in the same family fail or break themselves, while only the largest successfully self-improves.

💡 If self-rewriting scales, it dissolves the training/inference boundary and suggests a path to recursive self-improvement that could dramatically compress AI capability timelines — with downstream implications for AI-driven drug discovery, biology modeling, and the infrastructure stack built around fixed-weight deployment assumptions.
