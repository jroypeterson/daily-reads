Blackstone bets $1.5B on Pfizer's obesity drug (berobenatide)

BioPharma Dive · 2026-11-12 · ⏱ 3 min read

Blackstone is committing $1.5 billion to co-fund Pfizer's Phase 3 program for berobenatide, a monthly GLP-1 receptor agonist Pfizer acquired through its up-to-$10 billion purchase of Metsera. The deal is structured as royalty and milestone financing, representing a significant private capital bet on the obesity drug race and a de-risking mechanism for Pfizer's heavy pipeline investment. This arrangement signals growing appetite among alternative asset managers to participate in late-stage obesity drug development without taking on full equity risk.

💡 The Blackstone-Pfizer royalty structure sets a precedent for how big pharma can finance expensive GLP-1 Phase 3 programs with private capital, with direct read-through implications for Novo Nordisk, Eli Lilly, and smaller biotechs seeking non-dilutive funding for obesity pipeline assets.

Money Stuff: Nvidia's Board Had to Do Something

Bloomberg (Matt Levine) · 2026-11-12 · ⏱ 8 min read

Matt Levine dissects the corporate governance dynamics behind Nvidia's board decision to remove Jensen Huang as CEO following overlapping scandals — a relationship with a direct report and alleged ties to a Chinese intelligence-linked individual — framing the board's action through the lens of fiduciary duty and legal liability rather than mere moral judgment. Levine's central analytical thread is what 'had to do something' actually means: once a board becomes aware of material governance risk in a founder-led company, inaction itself becomes a liability. Secondary items touch on Anthropic's anchor investor structure and a macro hiring note.

💡 NVDA is a core AI infrastructure holding and Jensen Huang has been inseparable from the company's identity and strategic execution, making this succession and governance crisis a direct input for any portfolio with meaningful AI or semiconductor exposure.

🤖 Slot 3

Anthropic vs Meta: Room to Be Wrong

MBI Deep Dives (Substack) · 2026-11-12 · ⏱ 12 min read

MBI Deep Dives dissects the Anthropic IPO (rumored ~$1–1.5T valuation) against Meta's comparable enterprise value, decomposing each company's spending into 'core business' and 'speculative bet' buckets. The analysis reveals that ~60% of Meta's capex and a growing share of Anthropic's compute are funding products with unproven economics, while METR's independent assessment found Anthropic's AI capability progress nearly doubled in rate (~1.9x acceleration), with ~80% attributable to AI-driven R&D rather than raw compute growth. The piece frames the core investment question as how much valuation room for error each structure allows, using revenue-per-serving-dollar trends and R&D efficiency data to anchor the comparison.

💡 For investors sizing exposure across the AI ecosystem — particularly ahead of Anthropic's IPO and amid post-NVDA selloff sentiment shifts — this comparative framework offers a rare, data-grounded basis for evaluating relative risk/reward between a frontier lab at IPO and an incumbent big-tech AI infrastructure play.
