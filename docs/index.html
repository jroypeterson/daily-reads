Anthropic vs Meta: Room to Be Wrong

MBI Deep Dives (Substack) · 2026-11-09 · ⏱ 9 min read

MBI Deep Dives decomposes both Meta (~$1.5T EV) and Anthropic (~$1.5T pre-IPO) into a profitable core and a speculative AI bet, finding that ~60% of Meta's capex and ~75% of Anthropic's compute are directed at unproven products. The author uses Anthropic's S-1 filings and METR's external capability assessment (which found a 1.9x acceleration in AI research progress, ~80% attributable to AI itself) to show that while both companies are making massive bets, Meta's advertising core provides a margin of safety that Anthropic's pure API business lacks. The conclusion is that at equivalent valuations, Meta offers more 'room to be wrong' than Anthropic — making it the structurally superior holding.

💡 With Anthropic's IPO potentially pricing at $1–1.5T, this framework gives investors a rigorous basis for deciding between META exposure and a future ANTH position by quantifying exactly how much of each valuation is speculative AI optionality versus defensible core earnings.

Money Stuff: Is the Nvidia Affair Securities Fraud?

Bloomberg (Matt Levine) · 2026-11-09 · ⏱ 12 min read

Matt Levine dissects whether Jensen Huang's alleged undisclosed romantic relationship with a suspected Chinese spy — and Nvidia's reported intervention to suppress a Commerce Department investigation into chip diversion to China — constitutes securities fraud or material disclosure violations under SEC rules. He walks through the legal frameworks: Item 401/404 related-party disclosure rules, Rule 10b-5 materiality standards, and whether a CEO's personal relationship with a foreign national under counterintelligence scrutiny is the kind of information a reasonable investor would consider significant. Levine concludes the case is genuinely ambiguous but that the obstruction-of-government-investigation angle is more legally dangerous than the romance itself.

💡 NVDA is a core AI infrastructure holding and the combination of potential CEO departure, securities investigation, and national-security entanglement represents a material tail risk that any AI-exposed portfolio needs to price — Levine's framing gives investors the clearest legal map of what could actually happen next.
