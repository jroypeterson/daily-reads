Breaking: FDA approves Moderna's mRNA flu vaccine

STAT News · 2026-08-06 · ⏱ 4 min read

The FDA has approved Moderna's mFlusiva (mRNA-1010), the first mRNA-based influenza vaccine in the U.S., for adults 50 and older — with accelerated approval for those 65+ contingent on a postmarketing confirmatory study. The approval follows a turbulent regulatory path that included an unusual FDA refusal to review the application under Makary and Prasad, a subsequent reversal, and unanimous advisory committee support in June. Moderna expects the vaccine to be commercially available for the 2026-2027 flu season, with international reviews ongoing.

💡 This is a direct near-term catalyst for MRNA, validating the mRNA platform's commercial viability beyond COVID and opening a large flu vaccine market, while also signaling a broader shift in FDA posture toward previously stalled mRNA applications.

A digital health company became an insurance carrier under the thin veil of a product launch

The Health Care Blog · 2026-08-06 · ⏱ 6 min read

The author reverse-engineers how Progyny quietly transformed from a third-party fertility benefits administrator into a licensed insurance carrier over three years, using a trail of public signals — an actuary hire in 2023, a Washington state name reservation in August 2024, an insurance license granted by mid-2025, and active product filings — all predating the April 2026 'product launch' PR. The piece argues Progyny's decade of proprietary fertility claims data, Milliman-validated outcomes, and in-house PBM give it a credible actuarial edge to underwrite small employer fertility plans that incumbent insurers avoid. The author frames this as a generalizable framework for spotting stealth business model shifts in digital health companies before the market reprices them.

💡 Progyny's move from TPA margins to underwriter margins represents a structural valuation re-rating event that markets are still misreading as a simple downmarket expansion, and the four-signal detection framework (actuary hire, name reservation, insurance license, product filing) is directly reusable across other digital health SaaS names in the coverage universe.
