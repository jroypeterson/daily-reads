The Fruits of AI Drug Abundance: Indigestion In The Near Term

Life Sciences VC · 2026-10-30 · ⏱ 7 min read

A senior life sciences VC argues that AI in drug discovery will paradoxically worsen industry R&D productivity in the near term by generating a 'Drug Abundance' glut — flooding pipelines with me-too candidates against known, validated targets rather than cracking novel biology. Drawing on the 2001 Genomics Bubble precedent, the piece explains that AI's training sets are anchored in known drug-target space, driving crowding around hot targets (PD1, GLP, KRAS, etc.) at unprecedented scale. The structural bottleneck shifts from molecule generation to FDA review capacity, clinical trial infrastructure, and capital allocation discipline.

💡 For biotech investors, this reframes AI drug discovery hype as a crowding accelerant rather than a productivity unlock — with actionable implications for which players (CROs, CDMOs, regulatory infrastructure) benefit from pipeline overflow, and why competitive moats in AI-native drug startups may be weaker than consensus assumes.

🤖 Slot 3

Anthropic S-1: Race, Choice or Flywheel?

MBI Deep Dives · 2026-10-30 · ⏱ 14 min read

MBI Deep Dives tears apart Anthropic's S-1 to answer one question: what must be true for a buyer at a $1.5–2T IPO valuation to earn 10% IRR by 2030? The key S-1 revelation is Note 8, showing that customer-serving compute has fallen from 44% to 27% of total fleet — meaning three-quarters of the fleet is pointed at research/training, not revenue — while revenue per dollar of serving compute exploded 14x YoY in 1H'26. The piece then builds a granular model around fleet size (15GW by end-2028), serving-share assumptions, and revenue per serving watt to sketch a plausible path to ~$92B GAAP operating income by 2030.

💡 Anthropic's compute-allocation structure and inference margin trajectory are the defining inputs for valuing the entire frontier-model layer of AI infrastructure, with direct read-throughs to cloud hyperscaler capex, competing model providers, and healthcare-AI platforms built on Claude.

🌀 Slot 4

Medicare's AI-enabled prior authorization pilot linked to nearly 20% drop in some services

Becker's Payer Issues · 2026-10-30 · ⏱ 3 min read · 🔒 snippet-only (paywalled, verified on summary)

A JAMA research letter examining Medicare's WISR AI prior authorization pilot found a nearly 20% reduction in targeted services in participating states versus controls within the first six months, representing the first major real-world evidence of AI-driven prior auth impact at scale. The findings carry dual implications: managed care companies may see MLR improvement while hospitals face volume pressure, and a concurrent Pew survey showing 46% of Americans oppose AI in claims decisions signals meaningful regulatory and political headwind risk.

💡 This data point anchors investment theses across managed care (UNH, CVS, HUM), hospital systems, and health AI vendors by quantifying both the cost-reduction upside and the political/regulatory backlash risk that could constrain AI prior auth adoption.
