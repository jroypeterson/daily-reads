Money Stuff: The Agents Will Trade the Bonds

Bloomberg (Matt Levine) · 2026-11-18 · ⏱ 8 min read

Matt Levine explores the emerging reality of autonomous AI agents trading bonds and other financial instruments, using the concept of MEV (maximal extractable value) from DeFi as a lens to examine how agentic systems will reshape market microstructure, liquidity dynamics, and regulatory accountability in traditional fixed-income markets. He interrogates who bears liability when an AI agent executes a trade gone wrong, and how existing compliance and risk frameworks struggle to accommodate non-human decision-makers operating at machine speed. The piece is characteristically Levine: a specific emerging-tech development used to illuminate deep structural questions about how markets actually work.

🤖 Slot 3

A Layman Thinks About AI (Howard Marks / Oaktree memo)

Howard Marks memos (Oaktree / Brookfield) · 2026-11-18 · ⏱ 14 min read

Howard Marks applies his signature risk-first, second-level thinking to AI investing, organizing the memo around a series of hard questions: how AI actually works (citing Claude on interpretability gaps), what it will be able to do, who will capture value, and whether current valuations reflect realistic probability-weighted outcomes. He is characteristically skeptical of consensus bullishness, emphasizing the multiplicative uncertainty across technology evolution, competitive structure, demand forecasting, and timing. Rather than offering a buy/sell verdict, he frames AI as a domain where the incompleteness of investor knowledge is unusually high — and argues that acknowledging this is itself a form of edge.

💡 For a reader who prizes analytical depth and structural market insight, this memo is a rare document: a credible long-term capital allocator stress-testing the AI consensus narrative with a probabilistic framework, directly relevant to portfolio positioning in AI-adjacent equities.
