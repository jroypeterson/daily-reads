Merck KGaA bets on RNAi with $1.3B Silence buyout

BioPharma Dive · 2026-10-19 · ⏱ 3 min read

Merck KGaA is acquiring UK-based Silence Therapeutics for $1.3B (130% premium) to gain divesiran, an RNAi therapy in Phase 2 for polycythemia vera that analysts consider potentially best-in-class versus Takeda/Protagonist's approved Mimrylo. The deal follows Merck KGaA's Springworks buyout and reflects a strategic push into rare, high-specialization diseases using RNA modalities. Key shareholders representing 14% of voting power have agreed, with close expected Q1 2027.

💡 The 130% premium and 'best-in-class' analyst framing set a meaningful M&A comparable for other RNAi and rare blood disorder names, with direct read-throughs to Agios (developing a competing TMPRSS6-targeting drug) and the broader RNA therapeutics valuation landscape.

Shortage of Capital

The Transcript · 2026-10-19 · ⏱ 5 min read

The Transcript's weekly digest aggregates earnings call commentary from CEOs of Goldman Sachs, BlackRock, JPMorgan, Morgan Stanley, and others to frame a central macro thesis: for the first time in 50 years (per Larry Fink), there is a genuine shortage of capital as historically large government deficits collide with surging private AI infrastructure investment, driving rates higher. The consumer remains resilient and spending is strong, but sticky inflation and elevated asset prices are creating a volatile backdrop. Markets are fixated on how newly installed Fed Chair Kevin Warsh will respond to what he characterizes as a positive supply shock that is first hitting demand.

💡 A structural capital shortage regime that persistently elevates the risk-free rate is a direct headwind to long-duration assets — biotech pipelines, growth equities, and private market valuations — making this framework essential for sector allocation and discount rate assumptions across the coverage universe.

McKinsey: Pause, pivot, or accelerate: SaaS in the age of agentic AI

McKinsey · 2026-10-19 · ⏱ 6 min read · 🔒 snippet-only (paywalled, verified on summary)

McKinsey analyzes the strategic inflection point facing SaaS companies as agentic AI begins to restructure the software value chain, presenting a framework around three responses: pausing investment, pivoting the product model, or accelerating into AI. The piece draws on industry leader perspectives to identify which SaaS business models — particularly those reliant on per-seat licensing and workflow automation — are most vulnerable to agent-driven commoditization. Healthcare SaaS and health IT names are directly implicated as the shift redistributes value toward model providers and workflow owners.

💡 The structural threat to per-seat SaaS pricing from agentic AI has direct portfolio implications for health IT and digital health holdings, making this a strategically actionable read for investors assessing competitive moats in the sector.
